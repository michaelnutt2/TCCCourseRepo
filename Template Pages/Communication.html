Communication & Collaboration

A guide to working together effectively this semester.

Contacting Your Instructor

I am here to help you succeed. The best way to get in touch with me for non-technical questions is via email. To ensure a quick response, please follow these guidelines:

  • Use your TCC Email: Due to FERPA, I can only discuss grades and personal matters through your official my.tccd.edu email address.
  • Use a Clear Subject Line: Please start your subject line with our course number (e.g., COSC 1436: Question about Homework 3). This helps me find and respond to your email much faster.
  • Be Professional: Use a proper greeting, clearly state your question, and be respectful in your communication.

For all technical questions about code, Git, or assignments, please use the GitHub Issues system as outlined in our "How to Ask for Help" guide.

Group Collaboration Expectations

Working in a team is a skill, and our semester project is your chance to practice it. Successful teams are built on respect, communication, and accountability. All group members are expected to:

  • Communicate Proactively: Use your chosen communication channel (Discord, text, etc.) to provide regular updates on your progress and to notify your team if you are running into trouble or will be unavailable.
  • Participate Actively: Contribute to discussions, complete your assigned tasks on time, and provide thoughtful, constructive feedback during peer code reviews.
  • Be Respectful: Listen to your teammates' ideas, be open to different approaches, and handle disagreements professionally.
  • Uphold Your Role: Fulfill the duties of your assigned project role (Team Lead, etc.) for each phase of the project.
⚖️

Peer-Mediated Conflict Resolution

Disagreements are a normal part of teamwork. Our first step is always to try and resolve them within the group. However, if your group is truly stuck on a decision or is having an interpersonal issue, you can use our peer mediation process before escalating the issue to me.

  1. The group must agree to seek mediation.
  2. The Team Lead will respectfully ask a member from another project group to act as a neutral third-party mediator.
  3. The mediator's role is not to make a decision, but to listen to all sides and help the group find a compromise.
  4. If the issue cannot be resolved with a mediator, the Team Lead should then schedule a meeting with me.

Individual Accountability & Peer Feedback

To ensure that every member is contributing fairly to the group project, we will use a system of regular self and peer evaluations. This system is the primary tool for determining your "Individual Project Contribution" grade. After each major project check-in, you will be required to complete a short feedback form.

In this form, you will be asked to:

  • Briefly describe your own contributions since the last check-in.
  • Briefly describe the contributions of each of your teammates.
  • Provide a rating on how well the group is collaborating.

This feedback is confidential and will only be seen by me. It allows me to reward students who are active contributors and to intervene early if a group's dynamic is becoming unbalanced.
