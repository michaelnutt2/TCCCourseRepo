How to Ask for Help Effectively

A guide to getting unstuck like a professional.

The Golden Rule: Don't Suffer in Silence

Getting stuck is a normal and expected part of learning to code. The most important skill you can develop is learning how to get unstuck. I am always here to help you, but first, I want you to learn the process that professional developers use to solve problems and ask for help.

Our System: GitHub Issues

For all technical questions related to your code, Git, or Codespaces, we will use the "Issues" tab in your GitHub repository. This is the professional standard for tracking bugs and asking questions. Do not email me your code. Using GitHub Issues allows your teammates to help, creates a searchable history of solutions, and teaches you a critical industry skill.

The 3-Step Process to Getting Help

1.

Investigate on Your Own

Before creating an issue, take at least 15 minutes to investigate. This is not to discourage you, but to help you build problem-solving muscles. Try to:

  • Read the error message carefully. Copy and paste it into a search engine.
  • Re-read the relevant chapter in the book or the assignment instructions.
  • Explain the problem out loud to your duck (a technique called "rubber duck debugging").
2.

Create a Detailed GitHub Issue

If you're still stuck, go to the "Issues" tab in your GitHub repository and click "New Issue." You will see a "Bug Report" template. Fill out every section of the template completely. A good bug report is the key to getting a fast, helpful answer.

3.

Tag for Help

In the body of the issue, you can "tag" people to notify them.

  • If it's a question for your group, tag your teammates: @teammate-username
  • If you've already worked with your team and are still stuck, tag me: @michaelnutt2
