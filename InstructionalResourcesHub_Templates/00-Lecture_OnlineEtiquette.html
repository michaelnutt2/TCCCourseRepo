Introduction

In the digital age, maintaining proper online etiquette is crucial for effective and respectful communication. Whether interacting with instructors, classmates, or colleagues, adhering to established guidelines ensures a professional and courteous online presence. This page outlines key practices for online communication, including how to participate in discussion boards, send emails, and conduct yourself during virtual meetings.

Online Etiquette

General Guidelines

General Guidelines

When communicating online, you should always:

  • Respect your Instructor/s at all times whether in person or online, even in email or in any other form of communication.
  • Always address your Instructor/s with their proper title. You may call them Dr. or Professor, or if you are in doubt as to their correct title you may use Mr. or Ms..
  • Be sure to use clear and concise language when you communicate with others. Respect their time and attention.
  • Bear in mind that you are a College student and others expect you to use correct spelling and grammar in all your communications.
  • Avoid using slang words like “Wazzup?” and texting abbreviations like “u” instead of “you”
  • Always be mindful of your humor or sarcasm as it is sometime lost in an email or discussion post and your message might be taken literally and offensively.
  • Never share your and other person’s personal and sensitive information online without the person’s consent. But in sharing consented information, please be careful to whom you are sharing it with.

 

Discussion Boards

Discussion Boards

When posting on the Discussion Board in your online class, you should:

  • Only related and relevant posts that are on topic and within the scope of the course material are allowed to be posted.  If necessary, read the instructions of your instructor/s twice before making a move.
  • All your posts should be taken seriously. As much as possible write it in a word document, then, review and proofread your message before sending. You don’t want to be criticized for your incorrect grammar usage.
  • When you write a comment, be sure to be brief, concise, and direct while being detailed. Remember that you’re writing your comment in a discussion area, likened to a recitation, and you are not writing a thesis.
  • Always give proper credit to whom it is due when you use a source as reference material or directly quoting them. Never copy and paste or repeat another student’s post and claim it as your original because that is plagiarism.
  • When you agree on a certain post, don’t just say “I agree”, “I concur”, or “my answer is the same with...” You have to explain why you agree to a certain post.
  • Every opinion is as good as yours. Respect each other’s opinion even if their opinion is extremely different from yours. When you disagree with someone, you can express your opinion in a non-critical way. Don’t make it personal nor remark something insulting.
  • Always be open-minded especially that you are in an open environment.

 

E-Mail

E-Mail

When you send an email to your instructor, teaching assistant, or classmates, you should:

  • Always follow the format given by your Professor/s in writing the subject line. This is to give them a head-start of what to expect in your letter. Include your course, year and section and your issue/concern/suggestion in the subject line. Your Instructor may teach multiple courses and will have many students, so the more specific you are, the better and easier it will be for your professor.
  • When you write, be sure to be brief, concise, and direct while being detailed. Don’t make the reader scroll your entire message just to get a grasp of your concern.
  • Always write the significant topic at the beginning of your letter. Readers may be too busy to read the entire letter.
  • Avoid attachments unless you are sure that your recipients can open it. This is very important especially that some readers are only using smart phones or tablets to view emails.
  • Always put your name and your contact information including email at the end of your letter. Make sure they will know how to contact you back.
  • Think before you send your email to more than one person. If your message pertains only to one person, it would be better that you privately message that person instead of clicking “reply all.”
  • Be sure that you are forwarding the correct information before hitting the “forward” button.
  • Never wear your heart out when you are writing and sending email. It would be best that you have a rational mind in all your communications.

 

Teams Meetings

Teams Meetings

When attending an online meeting:

  • You are encouraged to find a place that is free from any distraction or noise where you can study properly online. If the same is not feasible, find a place that is most conducive to learning. Find a spot where the camera will be focusing on you alone and no other person and/or things could be in view. If the same could be impossible, students are encouraged to create a makeshift booth where they will be the only person in sight.

Summary

Online etiquette is essential for fostering a positive and respectful online environment. By following the guidelines provided, you can communicate effectively, avoid misunderstandings, and show respect for others' time and efforts. Proper online conduct not only reflects well on you but also contributes to a collaborative and productive digital space.
